WEEKLY RAMADAN RHYTHM FOR BEGINNERS Week 1: Adjustment and Awareness Focus: Body adjustment and basic structure  Learn fasting rhythm  Focus on intention and avoiding negativity  Keep prayers simple and honest  Light Qur‟an exposure (even listening) Key mindset: “I am learning. I am allowed to adjust

” Week 2: Stability and Consistency Focus: Routine and emotional balance  Try to be more consistent with prayers  Add short daily Qur‟an reading or listening  Work on controlling speech and reactions  Begin short daily reflection Key mindset: “Consistency is more important than intensity

” Week 3: Depth and Reflection Focus: Meaning and connection  Improve prayer focus, not length  Reflect on Qur‟an meaning  Increase du„ā‟, especially personal du„ā‟  Practice forgiveness and patience actively Key mindset: “I am building awareness, not chasing numbers

” Week 4: Completion and Renewal Focus: Intention and sincerity  Prepare for last ten days calmly  Increase night reflection within capacity  Focus on gratitude and forgiveness  Plan what to carry after Ramadan Key mindset: “Ramadan is shaping me, not exhausting me ” C. POST-RAMADAN CONTINUATION MODEL

1. What Continues (Minimum Sustainable Model)

After Ramadan, do not try to maintain Ramadan-level intensity. Maintain structure instead. Daily  One or more prayers consistently  One short du„ā‟  One moment of remembrance Weekly  One Qur‟an session (reading or listening)  One intentional good deed  One reflection moment This is success.

2. What Naturally

Reduces (And That Is OK)  Night prayers may reduce  Qur‟an quantity may decrease  Energy levels may normalize Reduction is not failure. Abandonment is the only loss.

3. Community and

Support  Stay connected, even lightly  One mosque visit a week or month  One trusted Muslim contact Isolation after Ramadan is common. Gentle connection prevents discouragement.

4. Mindset for Reverts

After Ramadan  Islam is not seasonal  Faith grows unevenly  Returning matters more than staying perfect If you fall behind:  Pause  Return  Continue This cycle is normal and accepted. D. Short Du‘ā’ Collection (Transliteration + Meaning) Du„ā‟ can be made in any language. These short supplications are provided to help beginners feel comfortable and confident.

Memorization is not required.

1. Du‘ā’ for

Intention and Sincerity Allahumma taqabbal minnī. O Allah, accept it from me.

2. Du‘ā’ for

Forgiveness Rabbighfir lī wa tub ‘alayya. My Lord, forgive me and accept my repentance.

3. Du‘ā’ for

Strength Allahumma a‘innī ‘alā dhikrika wa shukrika. O Allah, help me to remember You and to be grateful to You.

4. Du‘ā’ at

Iftar Allahumma innī laka sumtu wa ‘alayka tawakkalt. O Allah, I fasted for You and I rely upon You.

5. Du‘ā’ for

Ease Rabbi yassir wa lā tu‘assir. My Lord, make things easy and do not make them difficult. These may be read, listened to, or paraphrased in one‟s own words. E. Non-Arabic Prayer Cheat Sheet Prayer may feel overwhelming for new Muslims. This cheat sheet focuses on meaning first, helping establish connection before memorization. Basic Prayer Structure (Very Simplified)

1. Standing o

Intention in the heart o “Allahu Akbar” (God is greater)

2. Recitation o Any short chapter, verse, or remembered phrase o

If still learning, silent remembrance is acceptable

3. Bowing o

Bow and say: Subḥāna Rabbiyal ‘Aẓīm Glory be to my Lord, the Most Great

4. Prostration o

Say: Subḥāna Rabbiyal A‘lā Glory be to my Lord, the Most High

5. Ending o

Sit calmly o Make du„ā‟ in any language Important note for reverts: Prayer improves gradually. Understanding and sincerity matter more than perfect pronunciation. F.
